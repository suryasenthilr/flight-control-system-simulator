✈️ What is this Project? (Explain Like I'm 5)

You are looking at an Autopilot Simulation for an airplane, designed specifically for your Control Systems course!

🎯 1. What is the real-world story here?

Imagine you are sitting in an airplane cockpit. The pilot says: "Autopilot, pitch the nose UP by 10 degrees so we can climb!"
How does the airplane actually do that without wobbling, stalling, or crashing? It uses a Control System (Autopilot)! It measures where the plane is right now, calculates the error, and automatically moves the tail flap (elevator) up or down.

🖥️ 2. What are the 3 parts on your screen?

LEFT COLUMN ("The Remote Control"): You are the pilot! You pick the target pitch angle (e.g. +10°), and choose the brain of the autopilot (PID controller, Kp, Ki, Kd sliders).

CENTER COLUMN ("The Cockpit"): - Top: A 3D airplane. Look at the back tail—the amber elevator flap physically moves up/down in real time!
- Bottom: The Primary Flight Display (Artificial Horizon). Blue is the sky, brown is the ground.

RIGHT COLUMN ("The Control Systems Math"): - Time Scopes: The pink dashed line is your target (+10°). The bright blue line is the actual airplane. Watch the blue line chase and lock onto the pink line!
- Bode Plot: Frequency response showing Gain Margin & Phase Margin.
- S-Plane: Complex poles ($\sigma \pm j\omega$). If dots are in the green area (Left-Half Plane), the plane is stable! If they touch red, it crashes.
- Routh-Hurwitz: The exact stability table from your textbook calculated automatically.
